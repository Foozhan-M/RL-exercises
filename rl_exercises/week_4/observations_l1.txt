Observations:
    The baseline configuration (hidden_dim=64, buffer_capacity=10000, batch_size=32) learned successfully and achieved stable convergence after approximately 5000–8000 frames. The average reward increased gradually from around 10–20 during exploration to above 250 reward near the end of training.

    Effect of Batch Size
    Changing the batch size affected both learning stability and convergence speed. Smaller batch sizes (batch_size=16) produced noisier updates but still achieved strong final performance. Larger batch sizes (batch_size=128) resulted in smoother optimization and occasionally higher peak rewards, although learning tended to start more slowly during the early stages of training.

    Effect of Replay Buffer Capacity
    Replay buffer size had a noticeable effect on stability. A small replay buffer (buffer_capacity=1000) caused unstable learning and large performance drops after initially achieving high rewards. This is likely due to reduced transition diversity and overfitting to recent experiences. Larger replay buffers (buffer_capacity=50000) produced more stable training curves and smoother convergence behavior.

    Effect of Network Width (hidden_dim)
    Increasing the hidden dimension from 64 to 128 and 256 generally improved the representational capacity of the Q-network. Wider networks learned faster and often achieved higher peak rewards. However, larger networks also introduced slightly higher variance and instability during later stages of training.

    Combined Configurations
    The “all-low” configuration (hidden_dim=64, buffer_capacity=1000, batch_size=16) showed the least stable behavior and lower overall performance. In contrast, the “all-high” configuration (hidden_dim=256, buffer_capacity=50000, batch_size=128) achieved the highest peak reward during training, but also demonstrated some instability after rapid improvement.

    Overall, the experiments demonstrate that DQN performance is highly sensitive to replay buffer size, network capacity, and batch size. Larger replay buffers and wider networks generally improved performance, while extremely small buffers reduced training stability.
